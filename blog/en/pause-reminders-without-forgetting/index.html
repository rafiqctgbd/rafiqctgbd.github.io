Pause Your Reminders Without Forgetting to Turn Them Back On

A flight, an exam, a hospital stay, a long meeting: sometimes you need every alarm to stay quiet for a while. The real risk isn't pausing them. It's forgetting to switch them back on.

By SixSeasons Labs · Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

বাংলায় পড়ুন →

Most reminder apps offer a way to silence everything at once. It feels like a small convenience until you notice what usually happens next: you pause the alarms for an hour, life carries on, and the next morning you realise the electricity bill reminder never rang, because everything was still paused.

A pause that only ends when you remember to end it is a pause that quietly turns into "off". The fix is a pause with an end time.

When a pause makes sense

  • Travel. A flight, a long bus journey, or a different time zone for a day.
  • Somewhere you must be silent. An exam hall, a funeral, a hospital ward, a long meeting.
  • A day off. A holiday when you don't want weekday alarms going off at 7.
  • A child with your phone. Ten minutes of games shouldn't end in a ringing alarm.

The trap: "pause until I remember"

An open-ended pause depends entirely on your memory, which is exactly what a reminder app is supposed to replace. If you pause before a flight and land tired, the last thing on your mind is opening the app to resume it. A pause with an end time removes that step.

What a good pause should do

  • End by itself. Choose how long, and the alarms come back on their own, even with the app closed and even if the phone restarts in between.
  • Stay visible. While paused, something on screen should say so, and say until when, so a silent phone never surprises you.
  • Not flood you afterwards. Reminders that fell inside the pause shouldn't all fire at once, or be reported as "missed" when you chose to silence them.
  • Not swallow what comes right after. A reminder set for the exact moment the pause ends should still ring.

How to pause well

1. Pick the shortest pause that covers it

A two-hour meeting needs a three-hour pause, not "until I resume". If in doubt, choose the shorter option; you can always pause again.

2. Use "until tomorrow morning" for nights and travel days

It is the natural end for a late flight or a day you want to be left alone. Your morning reminders come back on time, without you doing anything.

3. Keep "until I resume" for when you really don't know

A hospital stay, a family emergency. Even then, the notification in your shade is there as a reminder that everything is still paused.

4. Check what's due before you pause

Glance at today's list first. If a bill is due during the pause, deal with it now, or pause for a shorter time.

What about medicine reminders?

Medicine & Health Reminder, NextCue's sister app, has the same timed pause, with the same choices. But be more careful with it: pausing a medicine reminder means no alarm for that dose. It makes sense in a hospital, where nurses give the medicines, or for a short time when you have already taken the dose. Doses inside the pause are not reported missed, so don't rely on the app's history to tell you what you skipped.

The short version

Pausing reminders is easy; remembering to switch them back on is the hard part. Give every pause an end time, pick the shortest one that covers what you need, and let the app bring your reminders back by itself.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if my phone restarts during a pause?

NextCue remembers when the pause ends. If the pause should still be running when the phone starts up, it is set up again; if the time ran out while the phone was off, NextCue resumes by itself once it starts.

What happens to reminders that fall inside the pause?

They don't ring, and they aren't reported as missed, because you chose to silence them. Repeating cues ring again at their next scheduled time.

Can I end a pause early?

Yes. Tap RESUME in the notification, or resume from the banner on the main screen. Every cue becomes active again straight away.

Does this cost anything?

No. NextCue and Medicine & Health Reminder are both completely free, with no ads.
