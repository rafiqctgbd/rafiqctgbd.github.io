Missed a Dose of Your Medicine? What to Do Now

The time has passed and the tablet is still in the strip. Take it now, or skip it? Most people get this wrong, not from carelessness, but because nobody ever explained it simply.

By SixSeasons Labs · Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

বাংলায় পড়ুন →

You glance at the clock and your stomach drops: the afternoon tablet still hasn't been taken, and it's nearly evening. Take it now? Skip it and wait for the next one? Would taking two together cause trouble?

At this moment most people either skip the dose out of worry, which isn't right for regular medicine like blood pressure or diabetes tablets, or take two at once thinking "a little extra can't hurt", which can be more dangerous. The problem isn't indecision. It's that nobody ever says plainly what to do.

⚠️ Read this first

This article is general information, not medical advice for any particular medicine. Rules differ from one medicine to another, especially insulin, blood thinners (such as warfarin), heart medicines and epilepsy medicines. If you are unsure about a specific medicine, ask your doctor or pharmacist directly. That is always the safest route.

The general rule: look at the gap

The rough rule that manufacturers and pharmacists usually give is to look at how close the next dose is.

  • If you remember fairly soon (less than half the gap between doses has passed), you can usually take it now.
  • If the next dose is close (more than half the gap has passed), it is usually safer to skip the missed dose and carry on with your normal schedule from the next one.

For example, a twice-daily medicine (every 12 hours) is due at 8 am and you remember at noon, 4 hours late: you can usually take it now. If you only remember at 6 pm, with the 8 pm dose two hours away, you would usually skip it and start again with the 8 pm dose.

Worth remembering

This is a general guideline, not an exact rule for every medicine. The leaflet inside the box often has a section headed "If you forget to take a dose" with specific instructions. It is worth reading once.

The most common mistake: taking two doses together

Worried about the missed dose, many people think, "I'll just take two now and make up for it." That is almost always the wrong decision. Two doses at once can push the level of medicine in your body up suddenly: dizziness or blood pressure dropping too far with blood pressure tablets, blood sugar falling too low (hypoglycaemia) with diabetes medicine, or stronger side effects with others. The rule: never take two doses together to make up for a missed one, unless your doctor specifically tells you to.

Different medicines, different rules

  • Regular blood pressure and diabetes medicine. One missed dose is usually not an immediate danger, but missing often can let blood pressure or sugar drift out of control over time. Consistency is what matters.
  • Antibiotics (a course). Take the missed dose as soon as you remember, unless the next one is close, and never stop the course halfway, even if you feel better. Stopping early raises the risk of resistant bacteria.
  • As-needed medicine (painkillers and the like). There is no such thing as a missed dose here; take it if you need it.
  • High-risk medicines: insulin, blood thinners, heart and epilepsy medicines. Don't guess. If you miss one, phone your doctor or pharmacist. A wrong guess costs too much.

Why it keeps happening

Missing one dose is not a failure. It is normal; anyone can miss one on a busy or distracted day. The real problem is when it keeps happening and no one notices how often. Without a written record, or one kept in an app, "now and then" can quietly become "three times a week", and nobody sees it until a blood pressure or sugar report shows the effect.

When to tell your doctor straight away

  • You missed insulin, a blood thinner, a heart medicine or an epilepsy medicine: ask, don't guess.
  • You have missed doses several days in a row: that isn't a one-off, it's a pattern your doctor should know about.
  • New or unusual symptoms after a missed dose, such as dizziness, unusual weakness or a racing heart: get in touch without delay.

The short version

After a missed dose, the safest path is to look at the gap, never take two doses together, ask rather than guess for high-risk medicines, and put something in place so it doesn't keep happening. Missing once isn't the problem; missing again and again without knowing it is.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take two doses together after missing one?

Usually not. Taking two doses at once to make up for a missed one can push the level of medicine up suddenly and raise the risk of side effects. Don't do it unless your doctor specifically says so.

How long after a missed dose is it safe to take it?

The general rule: if less than half the gap to the next dose has passed, you can usually take it now; if the next dose is close, skip the missed one and carry on with your normal schedule. For a specific medicine, the leaflet or your pharmacist is the safest guide.

What if I miss a dose of an antibiotic?

Take it as soon as you remember, unless the next dose is close. But don't stop the whole course because of a missed dose; finishing the course matters even when you feel better.

What if I miss insulin or a blood thinner?

Don't decide by guesswork with high-risk medicines like these. Phone your doctor or pharmacist straight away; a wrong guess can cost a lot.
