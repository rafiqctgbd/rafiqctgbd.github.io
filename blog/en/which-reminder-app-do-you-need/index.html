Choosing a Reminder App

Medicine & Health Reminder or NextCue: Which One Do You Need?

Both apps remind you of things, both are free, and both work in your own language. But they solve two very different problems, and picking the right one, or both, takes one honest question.

By SixSeasons Labs · Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

বাংলায় পড়ুন →

I build two reminder apps, Medicine & Health Reminder and NextCue, and the question I hear most is which one to install. It is a fair question: from the outside they look alike. Both ring an alarm at the right time, both survive a restart, both are free with no ads.

They are separate apps on purpose. Medicine needs a reminder several times a day that keeps count of every dose. Bills, renewals and birthdays need a reminder once a month or once a year, with a warning days ahead. Squeezing both into one app would make each of them worse.

Ask yourself one question

Do I, or someone I look after, take several medicines several times a day? If yes, start with Medicine & Health Reminder. If not (no regular medicine, or just one or two a day), the app you really need is NextCue, for bills, renewals, birthdays and the rest of life's dates.

💊 Medicine & Health Reminder is for you if

  • you take several medicines, more than once a day
  • you manage a long-term condition such as blood pressure, diabetes or heart disease
  • you look after an elderly parent's medicines
  • you need to track missed doses and keep health records

🔔 NextCue is for you if

  • you take no regular medicine, or only one or two a day
  • you need to remember bills, savings-scheme instalments or licence renewals
  • you want to track passport, insurance or vehicle-paper expiry dates
  • you want to remember once-a-year dates like birthdays and anniversaries

For many people the answer is "both": someone who takes medicine every day and is also the one who has to remember the household bills or the car's papers. Both apps are built on the same reliable alarm engine, so they sit side by side without getting in each other's way.

Medicine & Health Reminder, in six languages

English, Bengali, Hindi, Indonesian, Spanish and Arabic: pick one in Settings → 🌐 Language, whatever language your phone runs in. Exact-time medicine alarms, missed-dose tracking, a health tracker for weight, blood pressure and sugar, and medical records. Free, with no ads.

NextCue, in eight languages

English, Bengali, Hindi, Urdu, Arabic, Indonesian, Spanish and Portuguese. Start from 89 ready-made templates for bills, renewals, instalments and birthdays, every one of them translated. Free, with no ads, and available to everyone on Google Play.

Using either app in your own language

Neither app makes you change your whole phone's language. Each has its own language setting, so your phone can stay in English while the reminder app speaks Bengali:

  • Medicine & Health Reminder: Settings → 🌐 Language.
  • NextCue: Settings › App Language.

One exception: on some Xiaomi/Redmi phones running Android 10 with MIUI 12, the in-app language picker may not take effect. That is a limitation of MIUI, not of the apps; change the phone's own language instead. On Android 13 and later, and on other phones, it works as expected.

The short version

If you take several medicines a day, or look after someone who does, start with Medicine & Health Reminder. For bills, renewals and the year's important dates, NextCue. If you need both, install both: they are both free, and both speak your language.

Frequently asked questions

Should I install Medicine & Health Reminder or NextCue?

If you take several medicines a day, or look after someone who does, Medicine & Health Reminder. To remember bills, renewals or other dates in life, NextCue. If you need both, install both.

Are both apps free?

Yes. Both are completely free, with no ads and no in-app purchases.

Can I use the apps in Bengali while my phone stays in English?

Yes. Each app has its own language setting, separate from the phone's system language, so your phone can stay in English while these two apps are in Bengali.

Can I use both apps together?

Yes, and for many people that is the normal setup: one for medicines, the other for everything else in life. Both are built on the same reliable alarm engine and don't get in each other's way.
